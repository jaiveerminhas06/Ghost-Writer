Two ways Ghost Writer could look

Both start from the same subject: a detector reading a student's essay and admitting where it is unsure. They differ in which part of the writing world they borrow from.

Paper and margin

The one I picked

The essay is a typed page on a cool, blotter-grey desk. The detector writes in the page's margin in blue pen and marks sentences with highlighter, the way a careful teacher reads. One ruler from −10 to +10 carries every score, so the verdict is a position, not a badge.

Why paper and margin wins

  • Everyone already reads it. Highlighter and margin notes are a habit every student and teacher shares. Proofreaders' marks need a key, and your viva panel won't have one.
  • Blue is a comment, red is a correction. Red proof marks say "this is wrong", a verdict the detector can't support. Blue pen says "I noticed this", which matches your model card's own caveats.
  • It survives a projector. Atkinson Hyperlegible stays clear from the back of a room; Courier at body size doesn't.
  • It avoids a current AI default. The galley's narrow ruled columns sit close to the broadsheet look AI tools now reach for. Paper and margin doesn't.
