As a student in grade 8, I think summer projects should be student-designed because it allows us to be more creative and engaged in the learning process. When we have the freedom to choose our own project topics and design our own learning experiences, we are more likely to be motivated and interested in the work we are doing.

For example, if I am interested in science, I could choose to design a project that investigates the impact of different environmental factors on plant growth. I would be more invested in this project because it is something that I care about and am curious about, rather than being assigned a random topic by my teacher.

Moreover, when students are given the responsibility to design their own projects, it helps them develop important skills such as time management, critical thinking, and problem-solving. We are able to set our own deadlines and work at our own pace, which helps us develop a sense of independence and responsibility.

However, I understand that some students may struggle with designing their own projects and may need more guidance from their teachers. In these cases, it would be helpful for teachers to provide some initial suggestions and resources to help students get started.

In conclusion, while teacher-designed projects can be useful, I believe summer projects should be student-designed because it allows us to be more engaged and motivated in our learning experiences while also developing valuable skills.
