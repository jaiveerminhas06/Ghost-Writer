Summer projects are an excellent way to ensure that students continue learning during their break. The question remains whether these projects should be teacher-designed or student-designed. In my opinion, summer projects should be student-designed.

Firstly, student-designed projects allow for more creativity and autonomy. When students are given the freedom to choose their project, they can pick something that genuinely interests them. This leads to a more engaging and enjoyable learning experience. For instance, if a student is passionate about photography, they can design a project that involves taking pictures of different landmarks in their city. This way, they can develop their photography skills while also learning about their community.

Secondly, student-designed projects promote critical thinking and problem-solving skills. When students design their projects, they must brainstorm ideas, set goals, and develop a plan. This process helps them develop essential critical thinking skills, such as analyzing information, identifying patterns, and making connections. For example, a student who wants to start a school garden can design a project that involves researching the best plants to grow, creating a budget, and developing a plan to maintain the garden. This project will help the student develop critical thinking skills while also learning about sustainability and environmental responsibility.

Furthermore, student-designed projects encourage self-directed learning. When students are responsible for designing their projects, they must take initiative and work independently. This helps them develop self-directed learning skills, such as setting goals, managing time, and evaluating their progress. For instance, a student who wants to learn about coding can design a project that involves creating a website or an app. This project will help the student develop self-directed learning skills while also learning about computer programming.

In contrast, teacher-designed projects can be limiting and may not align with each student's interests and strengths. When teachers design projects, they may inadvertently impose their own biases and assumptions on the students. This can result in projects that are not engaging or meaningful to the students. For example, a teacher-designed project on medieval history may not resonate with a student who is more interested in science or technology.

In conclusion, summer projects should be student-designed. These projects promote creativity, critical thinking, problem-solving, and self-directed learning. By allowing students to design their projects, we can ensure that they continue learning in a way that is engaging, meaningful, and tailored to their individual interests and strengths.

Note: As a student in grade 12, I tried my best to provide reasons and examples to support my position. However, I recognize that there may be situations where teacher-designed projects are more appropriate, such as when students lack the necessary skills or resources to design their projects. In such cases, teachers can still provide guidance and support while allowing students to maintain some autonomy in their project design.
