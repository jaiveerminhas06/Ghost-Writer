Dear Senator,

I am writing to you today to express my strong support for abolishing the Electoral College and electing the President of the United States by popular vote. As you know, the Electoral College is a system in which each state is allocated a number of electors equal to the number of seats it has in the House of Representatives plus its two Senators. On Election Day, voters in each state actually cast their ballots for a slate of electors pledged to a particular candidate. The candidate who wins the popular vote in a state receives all of that state's electoral votes, with the exception of Maine and Nebraska, which award their electoral votes by congressional district.

The Electoral College was created at the 1787 Constitutional Convention as a compromise between those who wanted the President to be elected by Congress and those who wanted the President to be elected by a popular vote of the people. The Founders were concerned that a popular vote of the people would give too much power to the large states, and they also wanted to ensure that the President would be elected by a majority of the people, not just a plurality.

However, the Electoral College is no longer an effective system for electing the President. It is outdated, undemocratic, and unfair.

First, the Electoral College is outdated. It was created in a time when travel and communication were much slower, and when the United States was a much smaller country. In today's world, it is no longer necessary to have a system that gives more power to small states.

Second, the Electoral College is undemocratic. In a direct election of the President, each person's vote would count equally. However, in the Electoral College, a person's vote can count for more or less depending on the state in which they live. For example, in 2016, Hillary Clinton won the popular vote by nearly 3 million votes, but she lost the election because she did not win enough electoral votes.

Third, the Electoral College is unfair. It is possible for a candidate to win the popular vote but lose the election. This has happened five times in American history, most recently in 2016. When this happens, it means that millions of people have been denied a voice in the election.

For these reasons, I urge you to support abolishing the Electoral College and electing the President of the United States by popular vote. This would be a fair, democratic, and effective way to elect the President.
